# Part V — Track 4: STIs Beyond HIV

## Overview

While Track 1 covers HIV in depth, this track covers other common sexually transmitted infections — chlamydia, gonorrhea, syphilis, HPV, and herpes — building the same fact-based, stigma-reducing literacy for conditions that are far more common than most people realize, and often symptomless.

## The 10 Lessons

### Lesson 1: STIs: The Big Picture

**Learning goal:** Establish STIs as common, treatable, and not a moral issue.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- STIs are infections passed between people primarily through sexual contact.
- Many STIs are very common and highly treatable, especially when caught early.
- Having an STI is a health matter, not a reflection of someone's character.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Having an STI reflects a person's moral character. — True / **False ✓**

### Lesson 2: Chlamydia & Gonorrhea

**Learning goal:** Cover two of the most common bacterial STIs.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Both are bacterial infections and are treatable with antibiotics.
- Both frequently cause no symptoms at all, which is why regular testing matters.
- Left untreated, both can lead to more serious reproductive health complications over time.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Chlamydia and gonorrhea always cause obvious symptoms. — True / **False ✓**

### Lesson 3: Syphilis, Explained

**Learning goal:** Cover syphilis specifically due to its distinct stages and treatability.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Syphilis progresses through stages, and early symptoms can be mild or unnoticed.
- It is fully treatable with antibiotics, especially when caught in early stages.
- Untreated syphilis can cause serious long-term health complications.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Syphilis is treatable with antibiotics. — **True ✓** / False

### Lesson 4: HPV & Why It Matters

**Learning goal:** Introduce HPV, its prevalence, and prevention through vaccination.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- HPV (Human Papillomavirus) is extremely common — most sexually active people will encounter it at some point.
- A vaccine exists that protects against the HPV strains most linked to certain cancers.
- Most HPV infections clear on their own without causing problems.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Is there a vaccine that helps protect against HPV? — **Yes ✓** / No

### Lesson 5: Herpes: Facts Over Fear

**Learning goal:** Directly address the heavy stigma around herpes with factual information.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Herpes is a common viral infection that causes outbreaks but is manageable with treatment.
- Many people with herpes have mild or no symptoms and don't know they have it.
- It does not affect life expectancy and can be managed effectively long-term.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Herpes affects life expectancy. — True / **False ✓**

### Lesson 6: Why So Many STIs Have No Symptoms

**Learning goal:** Reinforce the core reason regular testing matters, across all STI types.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Many STIs, including chlamydia, gonorrhea, and HPV, often cause zero noticeable symptoms.
- This means someone can have and transmit an STI without knowing it.
- Regular testing — not waiting for symptoms — is the only reliable way to know your status.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. You can only have an STI if you notice symptoms. — True / **False ✓**

### Lesson 7: Prevention That Actually Works

**Learning goal:** Consolidate practical, evidence-based STI prevention strategies.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Condoms significantly reduce (though don't eliminate) risk for most STIs.
- Regular testing, especially with new or multiple partners, is a core prevention strategy.
- Open communication with partners about testing history is a normal, healthy practice.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Condoms eliminate all STI risk completely. — True / **False ✓**

### Lesson 8: Getting Tested & Treated

**Learning goal:** Demystify the practical process of STI testing and treatment.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- STI testing typically involves a simple swab, urine sample, or blood test depending on the STI.
- Most bacterial STIs are cured with a course of antibiotics.
- Viral STIs (like herpes and HPV) are managed rather than cured, but effectively controlled.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. All STIs are cured the same way. — True / **False ✓**

### Lesson 9: Talking to a Partner About STIs

**Learning goal:** Build practical communication confidence around a commonly avoided topic.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Bringing up testing history is a sign of respect for a partner's health, not an accusation.
- A simple, direct approach works better than an anxious or apologetic one.
- Normalizing this conversation reduces stigma for everyone involved.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Discussing STI testing with a partner is disrespectful. — True / **False ✓**

### Lesson 10: Bringing It Together

**Learning goal:** Consolidate the track with a mixed review before completion.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Recap: chlamydia, gonorrhea, syphilis, HPV, herpes, symptomless risk, prevention, and communication.
- This lesson mixes questions from Lessons 1–9 as a final check.
- Completing this lesson unlocks the track completion moment.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Mixed review — which STIs can be symptomless? **Many of them, including chlamydia and HPV ✓** / None — all STIs cause visible symptoms
- Q2. Mixed review — is regular testing important even without symptoms? **Yes ✓** / No

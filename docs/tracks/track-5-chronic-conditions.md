# Part VI — Track 5: Chronic Conditions (Sickle Cell & Diabetes)

## Overview

This track addresses two chronic conditions with major relevance in African contexts — sickle cell disease and diabetes — building basic literacy on causes, management, and daily life with these conditions, again with a focus on reducing stigma and misinformation.

## The 10 Lessons

### Lesson 1: What Is Sickle Cell Disease?

**Learning goal:** Establish the basic genetics and biology of sickle cell disease in plain language.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Sickle cell disease is an inherited blood disorder affecting red blood cells' shape and function.
- It is passed from parents to children through genes — it is not contagious.
- Sickle cell is especially prevalent in West Africa, making awareness particularly relevant.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Sickle cell disease is contagious. — True / **False ✓**

### Lesson 2: Sickle Cell Trait vs. Sickle Cell Disease

**Learning goal:** Clarify a commonly confused distinction with real family-planning relevance.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Having the sickle cell trait means carrying one copy of the gene, usually without symptoms.
- Having sickle cell disease means having two copies, which does cause symptoms.
- Knowing your trait/disease status matters for family planning decisions.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Sickle cell trait and sickle cell disease are the same thing. — True / **False ✓**

### Lesson 3: Living With Sickle Cell: Daily Management

**Learning goal:** Cover practical daily management strategies for sickle cell disease.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Staying well-hydrated and avoiding extreme temperatures can help reduce crisis frequency.
- Regular medical checkups help manage the condition proactively.
- With good management, people with sickle cell can live full, active lives.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. People with sickle cell disease cannot live full, active lives. — True / **False ✓**

### Lesson 4: Understanding Sickle Cell Crises

**Learning goal:** Explain what a pain crisis is and when to seek urgent care.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- A sickle cell crisis is an episode of severe pain caused by blocked blood flow.
- Triggers can include dehydration, extreme temperatures, or infection.
- Severe or prolonged crises need urgent medical attention.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. A sickle cell crisis can be triggered by dehydration. — **True ✓** / False

### Lesson 5: What Is Diabetes?

**Learning goal:** Establish the basic biology of diabetes and its two main types.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Diabetes affects how the body regulates blood sugar (glucose).
- Type 1 diabetes is typically diagnosed early in life and is not preventable; Type 2 often develops later and is linked to multiple factors.
- Both types are manageable with proper care.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Type 1 and Type 2 diabetes are exactly the same condition. — True / **False ✓**

### Lesson 6: Recognizing the Signs of Diabetes

**Learning goal:** Give practical, recognizable warning signs to encourage early testing.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Common signs include excessive thirst, frequent urination, fatigue, and unexplained weight change.
- Many people live with undiagnosed diabetes for years due to mild early symptoms.
- A simple blood test can confirm a diagnosis.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Diabetes always has severe, obvious symptoms. — True / **False ✓**

### Lesson 7: Living With Diabetes: Daily Management

**Learning goal:** Cover practical daily management strategies for diabetes.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Blood sugar monitoring helps guide daily decisions around food, activity, and medication.
- Balanced nutrition and regular physical activity support blood sugar management.
- With good management, diabetes does not have to limit a person's quality of life.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. With good management, diabetes must severely limit quality of life. — True / **False ✓**

### Lesson 8: Chronic Illness & Mental Health

**Learning goal:** Connect chronic condition management to emotional wellbeing, linking back to Track 3.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Managing a chronic condition long-term can be emotionally taxing, and that's a normal, valid experience.
- Burnout from constant self-management ("diabetes burnout" or similar) is a recognized phenomenon.
- Support communities and mental health resources (see [Track 3](./track-3-mental-health.md)) are a valid part of chronic condition care.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Feeling emotionally tired from managing a chronic condition long-term is unusual and abnormal. — True / **False ✓**

### Lesson 9: Supporting Someone With a Chronic Condition

**Learning goal:** Give practical, actionable guidance for friends and family.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Avoid making assumptions about what someone "can" or "can't" do based on their diagnosis alone.
- Simple practical support (understanding dietary needs, being flexible with plans) goes a long way.
- Respecting someone's own expertise about their own condition and body matters.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. It's appropriate to assume what someone can or can't do based only on their diagnosis. — True / **False ✓**

### Lesson 10: Bringing It Together

**Learning goal:** Consolidate the track with a mixed review before completion.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Recap: sickle cell trait vs. disease, crisis management, diabetes types and signs, daily management, and emotional wellbeing.
- This lesson mixes questions from Lessons 1–9 as a final check.
- Completing this lesson unlocks the track completion moment.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Mixed review — is sickle cell disease contagious? **No, it's inherited genetically ✓** / Yes, it spreads between people

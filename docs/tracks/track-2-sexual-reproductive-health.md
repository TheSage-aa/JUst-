# Part III — Track 2: Sexual & Reproductive Health

## Overview

This track extends Saabi's health-literacy mission into sexual and reproductive health (SRH) — puberty, contraception, consent, pregnancy, and reproductive rights — delivered with the same non-judgmental, bite-sized structure as Track 1. Designed for a general young-adult audience, not a clinical one.

## The 10 Lessons

### Lesson 1: Understanding Puberty & Your Body

**Learning goal:** Build a factual, shame-free understanding of the physical changes of puberty.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Puberty is a natural process where the body develops physically and hormonally into adulthood.
- Timing varies widely between individuals — there is no single "normal" age.
- Understanding your own body is the foundation for making informed health decisions later.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Puberty happens at the exact same age for everyone. — True / **False ✓**

### Lesson 2: The Menstrual Cycle, Explained

**Learning goal:** Give a clear, practical explanation of the menstrual cycle for all learners, regardless of gender.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- The menstrual cycle is typically 21–35 days and involves hormonal changes preparing the body for possible pregnancy.
- Irregular cycles are common and not automatically a sign of a problem.
- Understanding the cycle helps with both reproductive planning and general health awareness.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. A menstrual cycle length of 28 days is the only healthy length. — True / **False ✓**

### Lesson 3: Contraception: The Basic Options

**Learning goal:** Introduce the range of contraceptive methods available, without recommending one over another.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Options include condoms, hormonal methods (pills, injectables, implants), IUDs, and permanent methods.
- Effectiveness and side effects vary by method — this is a decision to make with a health provider.
- Condoms are the only method that also protects against STIs.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Which contraceptive method also protects against STIs? — **Condoms ✓** / The contraceptive pill

### Lesson 4: Consent — What It Actually Means

**Learning goal:** Establish a clear, practical definition of consent in relationships.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Consent must be freely given, ongoing, and can be withdrawn at any time.
- Silence or lack of resistance is not the same as consent.
- Consent given under pressure, fear, or manipulation is not real consent.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Can consent be withdrawn after it's initially given? — **Yes, at any time ✓** / No, once given it cannot change

### Lesson 5: Pregnancy: What Happens & When to Seek Care

**Learning goal:** Provide a factual overview of pregnancy stages and the importance of early antenatal care.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Early antenatal care significantly improves outcomes for both parent and baby.
- Common early signs of pregnancy include a missed period, fatigue, and nausea — but a test is the only way to confirm.
- Pregnancy affects every body differently; there is no single "normal" experience.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Why does early antenatal care matter? — **It significantly improves health outcomes ✓** / It has no real effect on outcomes

### Lesson 6: Reproductive Rights, In Plain Language

**Learning goal:** Introduce the concept of reproductive rights as basic human rights.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Reproductive rights include the right to accurate information, access to services, and bodily autonomy.
- These rights apply regardless of gender, marital status, or age of consent laws in a given context.
- Access to accurate information is itself a reproductive right.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Reproductive rights only apply to married adults. — True / **False ✓**

### Lesson 7: STIs & SRH: Where They Overlap

**Learning goal:** Bridge SRH knowledge with STI risk awareness without duplicating Track 4 in depth.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Many STIs are preventable with barrier methods like condoms.
- Some STIs have no visible symptoms, which is why regular testing matters even without symptoms.
- This track connects to Track 4 (STIs Beyond HIV) for a deeper dive.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Can you have an STI with no visible symptoms? — **Yes ✓** / No, there are always symptoms

### Lesson 8: Healthy Relationships & Communication

**Learning goal:** Connect SRH knowledge to practical relationship communication skills.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Talking openly about protection, boundaries, and testing is a sign of a healthy relationship, not a lack of trust.
- Avoiding these conversations out of awkwardness increases real risk.
- Practicing how to raise these topics makes it easier over time.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Talking about protection with a partner is a sign of distrust. — True / **False ✓**

### Lesson 9: Accessing SRH Services Confidentially

**Learning goal:** Reduce access-barrier fear by explaining confidentiality norms around SRH services.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Many SRH services are legally required to maintain confidentiality, including for younger patients in many contexts.
- Fear of judgment is one of the biggest reasons young people avoid SRH services.
- Asking a provider directly about their confidentiality policy is a reasonable and normal question.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Is it normal to ask a health provider about their confidentiality policy? — **Yes ✓** / No, it's considered rude

### Lesson 10: Bringing It Together

**Learning goal:** Consolidate the track with a mixed review before completion.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Recap: puberty, the menstrual cycle, contraception, consent, pregnancy basics, and reproductive rights.
- This lesson mixes questions from Lessons 1–9 as a final check.
- Completing this lesson unlocks the track completion moment.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Mixed review — which of these is a form of contraception that also prevents STIs? **Condoms ✓** / IUD
- Q2. Mixed review — consent can be withdrawn at any point. **True ✓** / False

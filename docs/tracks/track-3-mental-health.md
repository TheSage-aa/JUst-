# Part IV — Track 3: Mental Health

## Overview

This track introduces foundational mental health literacy — recognizing common conditions, building coping skills, and knowing when and how to seek help — with particular attention to the stigma young people in African contexts often face when discussing mental health.

## The 10 Lessons

### Lesson 1: What Mental Health Actually Means

**Learning goal:** Reframe mental health as a normal, universal part of overall health, not a fringe or shameful topic.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Mental health is part of everyone's overall health, not a separate or lesser category.
- Everyone has mental health, the same way everyone has physical health — it can be strong or struggling at different times.
- Having a mental health challenge is not a personal weakness or character flaw.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Mental health only applies to people with a diagnosed condition. — True / **False ✓**

### Lesson 2: Recognizing Anxiety

**Learning goal:** Give practical, recognizable signs of anxiety rather than clinical jargon.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Anxiety can show up as constant worry, restlessness, trouble sleeping, or physical symptoms like a racing heart.
- Occasional anxiety is normal; persistent anxiety that disrupts daily life is worth addressing.
- Anxiety is treatable, and support ranges from self-help techniques to professional care.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Occasional worry before a big event is always a sign of an anxiety disorder. — True / **False ✓**

### Lesson 3: Recognizing Depression

**Learning goal:** Give practical, recognizable signs of depression in accessible language.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Depression can look like persistent sadness, loss of interest in things you used to enjoy, low energy, or changes in sleep and appetite.
- Depression is a medical condition, not a lack of willpower or gratitude.
- Depression is treatable through therapy, lifestyle changes, medication, or a combination.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Depression is caused by a lack of willpower. — True / **False ✓**

### Lesson 4: Stress vs. Burnout

**Learning goal:** Distinguish everyday stress from burnout, a more serious and sustained state.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Stress is a short-term response to pressure; burnout is prolonged exhaustion from sustained, unmanaged stress.
- Burnout often includes cynicism, detachment, and a drop in effectiveness at things you're normally good at.
- Recognizing burnout early makes recovery faster and easier.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Burnout is the same as everyday, short-term stress. — True / **False ✓**

### Lesson 5: Healthy Coping Skills

**Learning goal:** Build a practical toolkit of evidence-informed coping strategies.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Coping skills include things like structured breathing exercises, physical movement, journaling, and talking to someone you trust.
- Different techniques work for different people — it's normal to try several before finding what helps.
- Coping skills support wellbeing but are not a replacement for professional care when needed.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. The same coping technique works equally well for everyone. — True / **False ✓**

### Lesson 6: The Weight of Stigma on Mental Health

**Learning goal:** Name the specific ways mental health stigma shows up and why it matters.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Mental health stigma often shows up as dismissiveness ("just get over it") or shame around seeking help.
- Stigma is one of the top reasons people delay seeking mental health support.
- Challenging stigma in everyday conversations creates safer environments for everyone.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Dismissing someone's mental health struggle can be a form of stigma. — **True ✓** / False

### Lesson 7: When and How to Seek Help

**Learning goal:** Provide practical guidance on recognizing when professional support is needed and how to start.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Seeking help is worth considering when symptoms persist for more than two weeks or interfere with daily life.
- Options range from school/university counselling services to community mental health providers.
- Reaching out for the first time is often the hardest step, and that's normal.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. You should only seek help once symptoms become extremely severe. — True / **False ✓**

### Lesson 8: Supporting a Friend

**Learning goal:** Give practical, actionable guidance for supporting someone else's mental health.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Listening without judgment is often more helpful than trying to "fix" the problem immediately.
- Encouraging professional support is appropriate, but you are not responsible for solving someone else's condition alone.
- Checking in consistently over time matters more than one big gesture.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. You are personally responsible for fixing a friend's mental health condition. — True / **False ✓**

### Lesson 9: Crisis Recognition & Immediate Safety

**Learning goal:** Give clear, direct guidance on recognizing a mental health crisis and the importance of immediate action.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Warning signs of a crisis include talk of hopelessness, giving away possessions, or expressing intent to harm oneself.
- In a crisis, prioritizing immediate safety and getting help right away matters more than waiting to "see if it passes."
- Knowing local crisis resources in advance makes it easier to act quickly if needed.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. If someone expresses intent to harm themselves, it should be taken seriously and acted on immediately. — **True ✓** / False

### Lesson 10: Bringing It Together

**Learning goal:** Consolidate the track with a mixed review before completion.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Recap: what mental health means, recognizing anxiety and depression, coping skills, stigma, seeking help, and supporting others.
- This lesson mixes questions from Lessons 1–9 as a final check.
- Completing this lesson unlocks the track completion moment.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Mixed review — persistent low mood and loss of interest in things you enjoy can be signs of: **Depression ✓** / Normal daily tiredness only
- Q2. Mixed review — is mental health stigma a real barrier to people seeking help? **Yes ✓** / No

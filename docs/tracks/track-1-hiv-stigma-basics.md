# Part II — Track 1: HIV & Stigma Basics

## Overview

This track is the first learning path in Saabi, built from LUMA's existing HIV and stigma content per the PRD (Section 4.1/7). It contains 10 lessons, sequenced from foundational facts to stigma and lived-experience content, each completable in under 2 minutes with a short quiz. Tone throughout: plain-language, non-judgmental, never clinical-cold and never fear-based.

## The 10 Lessons

### Lesson 1: What HIV Actually Is

**Learning goal:** Understand HIV as a virus that affects the immune system — replacing myths with the basic biology in plain language.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- HIV stands for Human Immunodeficiency Virus — it targets the immune system, specifically CD4 cells.
- HIV is not the same as AIDS. AIDS is a late stage that only happens if HIV goes untreated for a long time.
- With modern treatment, HIV very rarely progresses to AIDS at all.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. HIV and AIDS mean the same thing. — True / **False ✓**
- Q2. HIV mainly affects which part of the body? — The skin / **The immune system ✓** / The bones

### Lesson 2: How HIV Is Actually Transmitted

**Learning goal:** Replace vague fear with the specific, limited list of ways HIV spreads.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- HIV spreads through specific bodily fluids: blood, semen, vaginal fluids, and breast milk.
- It does not spread through saliva, sweat, tears, or casual contact.
- The most common routes are unprotected sex and shared needles.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Which of these can transmit HIV? — Sharing a cup / **Unprotected sex ✓** / Hugging
- Q2. HIV can spread through saliva. — True / **False ✓**

### Lesson 3: Myths vs. Facts

**Learning goal:** Directly debunk the most common HIV myths in a quick-fire format.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Myth: You can get HIV from mosquito bites. Fact: You cannot — HIV doesn't survive or replicate in insects.
- Myth: You can tell someone has HIV by looking at them. Fact: Most people with HIV look completely healthy, especially on treatment.
- Myth: HIV is a death sentence. Fact: People on treatment live long, full lives.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Can you get HIV from a mosquito bite? — Yes / **No ✓**
- Q2. Can you always tell if someone has HIV by looking at them? — Yes / **No ✓**

### Lesson 4: Testing — What to Expect

**Learning goal:** Demystify HIV testing to reduce fear-avoidance.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- A rapid test can give results in as little as 15–20 minutes.
- Testing is confidential, and many places offer free testing.
- Knowing your status — either way — is the first step to protecting your health.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. How long can a rapid HIV test take to give results? — Several weeks / **About 15–20 minutes ✓** / A full day

### Lesson 5: Treatment Today: ART & Undetectable

**Learning goal:** Introduce antiretroviral therapy and the concept of viral suppression.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- ART (antiretroviral therapy) is daily medication that controls HIV.
- With consistent treatment, HIV levels can become "undetectable" in the blood.
- Undetectable means the virus is suppressed to the point it can't be measured by standard tests.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. What does ART stand for? — **Antiretroviral Therapy ✓** / Automatic Response Test

### Lesson 6: U=U: Undetectable = Untransmittable

**Learning goal:** Teach the single most stigma-reducing fact in modern HIV science.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- U=U is backed by major global health bodies including the WHO.
- A person on effective treatment with an undetectable viral load cannot sexually transmit HIV to a partner.
- This single fact has changed how the world understands living with HIV.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. What does U=U stand for? — Understood = Universal / **Undetectable = Untransmittable ✓**
- Q2. Someone who is undetectable can still transmit HIV sexually. — True / **False ✓**

### Lesson 7: What Stigma Actually Looks Like

**Learning goal:** Name stigma in concrete, recognizable everyday forms rather than an abstract concept.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Stigma can be a joke, an assumption, an awkward silence, or someone being excluded.
- It often comes from outdated fear, not current facts.
- Stigma is one of the biggest reasons people avoid getting tested.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Which of these is an example of stigma? — **Avoiding someone because of an assumption about their status ✓** / Asking a doctor a health question

### Lesson 8: The Cost of Silence

**Learning goal:** Connect stigma directly to real-world harm — testing avoidance, isolation, mental health.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Fear of stigma is one of the top reasons people delay or avoid HIV testing.
- Silence and shame can be more damaging, day to day, than the virus itself when someone is on treatment.
- Breaking silence starts with accurate information — which is what this track is for.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Fear of stigma can make people avoid testing. — **True ✓** / False

### Lesson 9: Supporting Someone Living With HIV

**Learning goal:** Give practical, actionable guidance for allies.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Treat someone's status as private health information — not something to share without consent.
- You don't need to change how you interact with someone based on their status.
- Simple, consistent respect is more valuable than grand gestures.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. If a friend shares their HIV status with you, what should you do? — **Keep it private unless they say otherwise ✓** / Tell close mutual friends so they're careful

### Lesson 10: Bringing It Together

**Learning goal:** Consolidate the track with a mixed review before completion.

**Lesson content (short-form, <2 min read)**
- Recap: transmission facts, testing, treatment, U=U, and what stigma really looks like.
- This lesson mixes questions from Lessons 1–9 as a final check.
- Completing this lesson unlocks the track completion moment.

**Quiz questions**
- Q1. Mixed review — U=U means someone on effective treatment: **Cannot sexually transmit HIV ✓** / Is fully cured
- Q2. Mixed review — the biggest driver of HIV testing avoidance is usually: **Stigma and fear ✓** / Cost of the test
